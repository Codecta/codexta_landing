Authoring: change what the assistant knows and does

Authoring lets you change the rules your store’s assistant follows, and the tools and chat cards it uses. Describe the change in a chat or make it yourself in the Code tab, try it on a branch, and it goes live only when you say so. It works the same in every shop Codexta connects to, and a change applies to your store only.

Places are written as Codexta › Assistant › Changes. In Shopify, Codexta is under Shopify admin › Apps › Codexta; in WooCommerce, under WooCommerce › Codexta Assistant. Things you can type in the chat look like “Show me the shopping guideline”. Back to the Shopify guide or the WooCommerce guide. New words are explained in the glossary.

01

What authoring is

The assistant works from guidelines: written rules for one kind of work, such as the shopping guideline (search, cart, checkout, orders) or the back-office guideline (orders, customers, products). It acts through tools (search products, add to cart, read an order) and shows results as cards (product cards, the basket). Codexta ships all three. Authoring lets you keep your own copy of one and change it, or add a new one, without touching any other shop.

  • Your store only. Every change is written to your store’s own folder. Other stores and Codexta’s originals never change.
  • Prepared as a draft. You work on a separate draft copy, called a branch. Your shoppers keep getting the live version until you publish.
  • You decide when it goes live. Approving a draft is not publishing. Nothing goes live until you press Publish or ask the assistant to publish.
  • Code is checked by people. A change to a tool or a card runs only after Codexta has read and approved that exact code (Codexta review). Guideline and knowledge-base changes need no review.
  • Two ways to work. Describe the change in the Authoring chat and the assistant writes it, or open Assistant › Code and change it yourself in an editor with named fields. Both work on the same branch.
  • Who: the Owner and Admins. KB editors, Support managers, Support agents and Viewers do not see authoring.
  • Plan: authoring is not on every plan. Branches and test mode for knowledge-base drafts, and switching guidelines on or off, are on every plan.

For a single habit such as “ask for the budget before suggesting a gift”, a lesson is quicker: tell the Store assistant and it applies it from the next chat (see “Teach the assistant” in the Shopify or WooCommerce guide). Use authoring when you want to change how a whole guideline reads or works, or what a tool or card does.

Words on this page. Branch: your draft copy. Build: the automatic check of a change; green means it passed, red means it has a mistake. Commit: save a set of edits on the branch with a short message. Publish (also “merge”): make the draft live. main: the live version your shoppers get. Diff: the list of lines added and removed. Frontmatter: the settings block at the top of a guideline file. More words in the docs glossary.

03

Guideline, tool or card: which to change

The three work together. When a shopper writes, the assistant picks the guideline whose “When to use it” fits the question. That guideline brings its tools: the assistant can call only tools that are offered with a loaded guideline. A tool can show its result as a card. So most changes are guideline changes; you change a tool or card only when the assistant cannot do or show what you need.

You want to…ChangeWhy
Say something different, follow a policy, ask a question first, keep answers shorterThe guideline’s textThe guideline is what the assistant reads before it answers. No review, live when you publish.
Make the assistant use a guideline for more (or fewer) questionsThe guideline’s When to use it and Example questionsThat text decides when the guideline is loaded at all.
Cover a new topic, such as gift wrapping or a loyalty programmeA new guideline of your ownA topic of its own loads only when it fits, and keeps the shop guideline short.
Let a topic use an existing action, such as adding to the cartThe tool’s Offered with guidelinesA tool is available only while one of its guidelines is loaded.
Change what a tool is used for, or what shoppers read while it runsThe tool’s What it does, While running, When doneThe assistant reads “What it does” to decide when to call it; the two short texts are what shoppers see.
Fetch other data, or do something the shop can do but the assistant cannotA tool’s code, or a new toolOnly code can call your shop. Needs Codexta review.
Change how a result looks in the chatA cardCards decide layout, labels and buttons. Needs Codexta review.

Start an authoring chat

How do I open authoring?

  • Who: Owner, Admin
  • Plan: not on every plan

Codexta › Assistant › Chat

  1. If a chat is open, press New chat.
  2. At the top, choose Authoring. The other choices are Store assistant (your back-office helper), the assistants of your channels (Messenger, Instagram, WhatsApp and so on) and Shopping assistant, which shows what your shoppers get.
  3. The chat greets you with Describe the capability you want to add or change. Write what you want in your own words, in any language.

The choice is fixed once the chat starts. To switch, press New chat.

06

Change a rule for shoppers

How do I change what the assistant tells shoppers?

  • Who: Owner, Admin
  • Plan: not on every plan

Codexta › Assistant › Chat › Authoring

  1. Describe the rule as you would to a new colleague. For example:
    YOU WRITEWhen a shopper asks about delivery, always mention that shipping is free over the amount in our shipping note.
  2. The assistant finds the guideline that governs delivery questions, makes your store’s copy of it, and changes only that passage. Everything else in the guideline stays word for word.
  3. It checks the change (the build) and tells you whether it is ready. This takes seconds.
    YOU SEE (EXAMPLE)I added the rule to your copy of the shop guideline, under delivery. The build is green. Try it here: ask “How much is delivery?” the way a shopper would. When you are happy, say “publish it”.
  4. Try it, then publish it.

More things you can ask for:

  • “Never promise a delivery date; say when the order usually ships instead.”
  • “Offer gift wrapping when the basket is over 50.”
  • “When you show more than three products, end with one question that narrows the choice.”
  • “Keep product answers to two sentences plus the cards.”

If a request is unclear, the assistant asks once, with concrete options, before it writes anything. It refuses rules that would change permissions, plans or other stores. To make the same change by hand, see example A.

07

Add a guideline of your own

How do I add a new guideline?

  • Who: Owner, Admin
  • Plan: not on every plan

Codexta › Assistant › Chat › Authoring, or Assistant › Code › your store’s guidelines folder › +

Use a new guideline for a topic no Codexta guideline covers. It can serve your shoppers or your team, and it is used in every chat of your store that can see your platform’s guidelines.

YOU WRITEAdd a guideline for gift wrapping: it costs 4 EUR per order, the shopper can add a gift message to the order note, and wrapped orders ship one working day later.
  1. The assistant checks first whether an existing guideline already covers it, and if so offers to change that one.
  2. It writes the new guideline with a short When to use it, so the assistant knows when to load it, and builds it.
  3. It tells you which tools the guideline needs (here: adding to the cart) and offers to make them available with it (what is bound to what).
  4. Try it on the branch, then publish.

Every guideline of your own carries requires: platform=… with your shop’s platform (for example platform=woocommerce). The editor adds it for you and shows it locked as Who it is for; a guideline without it is refused, so a store’s guideline can never reach another platform. The whole walk-through is example B.

08

Branches and test mode

How do I start a branch?

  • Who: Owner, Admin
  • Plan: any

Codexta › Assistant › Chat (the bar at the top)

Without a branch, the bar reads Chats here use the live knowledge base. Work on a branch to chat with your drafts before shoppers see them.

  1. Press Work on a branch, choose New branch… in the list, type a name (lowercase letters, digits and dashes, for example summer-returns) and press Create.
  2. The bar turns yellow: Branch: summer-returns · Changes here are drafts; shoppers don't see them. From now on, the Chat, the Knowledge base and the Code tab all work on this branch.
  3. To leave it without losing anything, press Back to live. Pick it again from the list later.

How do I see what is on my branch?

  • Who: Owner, Admin
  • Plan: any

Codexta › Assistant › Chat › Changes (n)

Changes (n) lists every draft on the branch: Added or Changed, the file, and Edit in Assistant › Code. Knowledge-base drafts also have Revert.

  • Publish: make the branch live (below). If an article changed live after your draft started, publishing stops and names it: revert that draft or edit it again first.
  • Discard: delete the branch and its drafts. The live version stays as it is.
  • A yellow line This branch has changes that are not built yet… means the chat still uses the branch’s last build: press Build in Assistant › Code.

What is test mode?

  • Who: Owner, Admin
  • Plan: any

Codexta › Assistant › Chat › Test mode (no real orders, emails or shop changes)

Tick it to try a chat safely: the assistant goes through the same steps, but tools that would place an order, send an email or change your shop only say what they would have done. It is separate from branches: use it on a branch to try your drafts, or on live to try what shoppers get. Lessons and imports you make in the chat stay real.

Can I draft knowledge-base articles?

  • Who: Owner, Admin
  • Plan: any

Codexta › Knowledge base (with a branch open)

Yes. Without a branch, Changes to articles go live right away. Work on a branch to try them as drafts first. With a branch open, every article you write, edit or delete is a draft on that branch: chats on the branch answer from the drafts, shoppers keep the live articles, and Publish puts all drafts live together.

09

Try a change before it goes live

How do I test a change?

  • Who: Owner, Admin
  • Plan: not on every plan

Codexta › Assistant › Chat (with the branch open)

  1. Make sure the branch is built green (the status bar in Assistant › Code reads head green). Until then, chats on the branch use its last green build.
  2. Tick Test mode if the test would place an order or change your shop.
  3. Choose the assistant that runs the change (for a shopper rule: Shopping assistant) and ask a question it governs, the way a shopper would, for example “Can you gift wrap my order?”. This chat uses your branch; your shoppers never see it.
  4. If the answer is not right, change the guideline (or ask the Authoring chat to), build again and ask again.

To see exactly what changed, ask “Show me the diff”, or click a file under Changes vs main in Source Control. Lines added are marked +, lines removed −.

Your own tools and cards run on the branch only after Codexta approved them; until then the branch chats use the shared ones (Codexta review).

10

Publish a change

How do I make a change live?

  • Who: Owner, Admin
  • Plan: not on every plan

Codexta › Assistant › Chat › Publish, or Assistant › Code › Source Control › Merge or send for review

  1. Press Publish in the branch bar, or in the Code tab Merge or send for review, then Merge in the dialog. In the Authoring chat, say “Publish it”: saying yes to a draft is not enough.
  2. Guideline and knowledge-base changes go live for your store at once, from each conversation’s next message.
  3. A change that holds code (a tool or card) is sent to Codexta for review instead, and nothing is merged until Codexta approves it (below).
  4. The change is listed under Assistant › Changes › Who changed what.
11

Undo a change

How do I go back?

  • Who: Owner, Admin
  • Plan: not on every plan (switching off: any plan)

Codexta › Assistant › Chat › Authoring, or Assistant › Code

  • Go back to Codexta’s version: delete your copy (in the Code tab, the bin icon next to the file, on a branch) or say “Remove my copy of the shop guideline and use Codexta’s again”. Publish it like any other change.
  • Undo one rule: “Take out the free-shipping rule I added”, or edit the copy. Every earlier version is kept in the history.
  • Throw a draft away: Discard in the branch bar. Nothing live changes.
  • Stop it right away: switch your own guideline or tool off under Assistant › Guidelines (below). This takes a few seconds and needs no build.
12

Switch a guideline on or off

How do I switch a guideline off for my store?

  • Who: Owner, Admin
  • Plan: any

Codexta › Assistant › Guidelines

  1. The page lists every guideline your assistant runs, grouped by where it is used (Store assistant, Shopping assistant), each with its description and an On / Off switch. Guidelines of your own are listed too.
  2. Untick the switch. If other guidelines that are on still use it, you are asked first: These guidelines are on and still use “…”. Turn it off anyway?
  3. The switch saves in a few seconds and applies to your store only. No guideline text changes.

A guideline you have switched shows default: on (or off) and a Reset link, which removes your switch and goes back to the default.

How do I switch one tool off for my store?

  • Who: Owner, Admin
  • Plan: any

Codexta › Assistant › Guidelines

  1. Under a guideline, open Tools (n/m on). It lists the tools that guideline brings for your platform, each with a short description.
  2. Untick a tool to switch it off, tick it to switch it back on. It saves in a few seconds and applies to your store only.

A switched-off tool is no longer offered to your assistant. If a conversation still tries to use it, the call is refused and the assistant carries on without it. The guideline itself stays on. A tool that several guidelines bring has one switch, shown under each of them. The tools every conversation needs, such as asking the shopper a question, planning and the knowledge base, are not listed, so they cannot be switched off.

13

Take Codexta’s updates

What happens when Codexta improves a guideline I copied?

  • Who: Owner, Admin
  • Plan: any

Codexta › Assistant › Changes › Updates from Codexta

Your copy does not change by itself. Every copy remembers what it was copied from (its Based on line in the Settings panel). When Codexta changes the original, the guideline shows update available in this panel (copies you edited are marked edited by your store).

  1. Press View changes to see what Codexta changed since your copy was made.
  2. Choose one:
    • Take the update: your copy becomes Codexta’s new version. Your own edits in it are replaced; your on/off switch is kept.
    • Use Codexta’s version (remove my copy): your copy is removed and the switch goes back to Codexta’s default.
    • Keep mine: keep your text and dismiss this update.

If you took an update and want your rule back, add it again to the new version (or ask the Authoring chat to).

15

The Code tab

Assistant › Code is an editor for your store’s branches, laid out like a code editor. The Authoring chat does the same work by conversation; the Code tab is for reading a change line by line or making it yourself.

What is where?

  • Who: Owner, Admin
  • Plan: not on every plan

Codexta › Assistant › Code

  • Left bar: Explorer (files), Search, Source Control and Problems (build findings; the badge counts them).
  • Explorer: only what your store can use.
    • guidelines: Codexta’s guidelines for your platform (another platform’s, such as Shopify’s for a WooCommerce shop, are not shown).
    • present: the cards the tools of your platform show.
    • tools/<your platform> and libs: Codexta’s tools for your platform and the code they use, to read and copy.
    • tenants/<your store>: your store’s own folder, with guidelines, kb (knowledge-base drafts), present (cards) and tools. Everything you change lives here.
    • sdk (locked): reference of what tool code may use.
  • New files: hover a folder of your store and press the + next to it. The new file starts from a template for that folder: a guideline with its settings (and requires: platform=…), an article, a tool or a card.
  • Tab bar: open files; Save (or Saved; the shortcut is Ctrl+S or ⌘S); Settings shows or hides the Settings panel; cards also have Preview.
  • Status bar (bottom): the branch (press it to switch or create one), draft, the build state (head green, head red, head not built, “+ edits” when there are unsaved-to-head changes, and last green), Build, and the problem counts.

main is what your shoppers run; it is read-only and changes only by publishing a branch. On main you see: main changes only by merge. Create a branch from the status bar to make changes.

How do I change one of Codexta’s files for my store?

  • Who: Owner, Admin
  • Plan: not on every plan

Codexta › Assistant › Code (on a branch)

  1. Open the file. A shared file shows a lock and a bar: Shared guideline, read-only. Copy it to your store to change it for your store only; your copy is used as soon as you merge it. (For a tool or card: …your copy runs once Codexta approves it.)
  2. Press Copy to my store. The copy opens from your store’s folder, ready to edit. A guideline copy remembers what it was copied from; a tool copy is moved into your store’s own namespace.
  3. If your store already has a copy, the button reads Open my copy and takes you to it in the tree.

How do I save, commit and build?

  • Who: Owner, Admin
  • Plan: not on every plan

Codexta › Assistant › Code › Source Control

  1. Save (or Ctrl+S / ⌘S) writes the file to the branch.
  2. In Source Control, write a short message and press Commit (or Cmd+Enter). Changes lists what is not committed yet; Changes vs main lists what publishing would bring (click a file for its diff).
  3. Press Build (next to Commit, or in the status bar). In seconds the status bar shows head green or head red; a red build lists its mistakes under Problems, each with the file and line.
  4. Merge main into branch brings Codexta’s and your other published changes into a long-running branch. Merge or send for review publishes (above).

The Settings panel

Every guideline and tool has settings: its title, when it is used, which tools it comes with, and so on. In the file they are a small block of text (a guideline’s frontmatter, a tool’s [AgentTool(…)] line), where one stray character could break them. So the editor folds that block into one grey line, ⚙ Settings (title, when to use, examples…): edit them in the Settings panel, which you cannot type into, and shows the settings as named fields on the right. What you change in the panel is written into the file exactly as the assistant reads it; save and build as for any edit.

17

What is bound to what

Under the tab bar, one line shows what the open file is bound to, from the branch’s last build. Every name opens its file.

  • A guideline: Used by 11 tools: and the tools that come with it. Your store’s tools are highlighted; tools built into Codexta (the knowledge base, asking the shopper, and so on) are grouped as +7 built-in; click it for the full list.
  • A tool file: 5 tools, offered with and the guidelines and cards its tools use.
  • A card: Shown by and the tools that show it.
  • Warnings on the same line: no tool comes with this guideline, a tool with no guideline is never offered, no tool shows this card. save and build to update means the line still shows the last build.

The binding is always set on the tool (Offered with guidelines), never in the guideline. A guideline can mention a tool by name in its text, but only the tool’s setting makes it available.

18

Your own tools and cards

Tools are what the assistant can do in your shop. Cards are how results look in the chat. Because both run code inside Codexta, your versions run only after Codexta has approved that exact code.

  • Copy and change: open a tool in tools/<your platform> or a card in present and press Copy to my store. Change its settings in the panel, or its code. Your copy replaces Codexta’s for your store; every other store keeps Codexta’s.
  • New: press + on your store’s tools folder (a C# tool) or present folder (a card).
  • What tool code may use: plain computation, text, JSON, your platform’s shop library (libs/<your platform>), the tool context, and the secrets and settings Codexta gave the tool. No network calls of its own, files, processes or another platform’s code; the build refuses them with the reason.
  • Namespace: every class in your folder lives in your store’s namespace, Tenant_<your store id>. Copies and templates already have it; the build refuses any other.
  • Cards: use Preview to see the card next to its code.

Until Codexta approves your code, chats (also on your branch) use Codexta’s shared tools and cards. A tool of your own with no shared counterpart answers waiting for Codexta approval.

19

Codexta review

How does my code get approved?

  • Who: Owner, Admin
  • Plan: not on every plan

Codexta › Assistant › Code › Source Control

When your branch holds code, Source Control shows Your store's code on this branch runs only after Codexta approves it.

  1. Press Send for Codexta review. It commits and builds the branch, then sends its code to Codexta without publishing. (Merge or send for review does the same when the branch holds code.)
  2. The box reads Waiting for Codexta review. The branch is frozen until Codexta decides; chats on it use the shared tools meanwhile.
  3. Codexta reads the diff of exactly that commit, file by file, and decides:
    • Approved: Codexta approved the code of commit … It runs for your store; any later change to it needs a new review. Your code now runs on the branch: try it, then publish it yourself.
    • Merged by Codexta: approved and published in one step.
    • Refused: Codexta refused this code: and the reason. Change the code and send it again.

Approval is for that exact code. Changing one character of an approved tool or card needs a new review; guideline and article changes on the same branch never do.

20

Worked examples

Five changes a shop typically makes, from the smallest to the largest. Each starts on a branch (Work on a branch) and ends with a test on that branch and Publish.

A. Add your phone hours to the contact guideline

  • Changes: one guideline
  • Review: no

Why a guideline: the assistant already knows how to put a shopper in touch; it only needs your policy. That is text the assistant reads, not something it does.

  1. Code tab › guidelines/contact_us.md › Copy to my store.
  2. In the Settings panel, add a line to Example questions: When can I call you?
  3. At the end of the text, add the rule: 
    ## Phone hours

Our phone line is open Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 17:00. Outside those hours,
offer the contact form and say we answer the next working day.
  4. Save, commit, build. In the Chat tab ask, as a shopper would: “Can I call you on Saturday?”. The answer should name the hours and offer the form.
  5. Publish. No review needed.

In the file, the example question lands in the frontmatter as examples: Contact support, I want to report an issue, When can I call you?, …; you never edit that line by hand.

B. A gift-wrapping guideline that can add wrapping to the cart

  • Changes: a new guideline, one tool setting
  • Review: yes (the tool)

Why a new guideline: gift wrapping is a topic of its own with its own questions. As a separate guideline it is loaded only when a shopper asks about wrapping, and the shop guideline stays as it is. Why a tool setting: the new guideline tells the assistant to add wrapping to the cart, but the assistant can use woocommerce_cart_add only while a guideline it is offered with is loaded. So the tool must be offered with gift_wrapping too.

  1. Hover tenants/<your store>/guidelines, press +, name it gift_wrapping.md.
  2. Fill the Settings panel: Title Gift wrapping; When to use it Gift wrapping and gift messages: a shopper asks whether an order can be wrapped, what it costs, or how to add a message. NOT returns of a gift.; Example questions, one per line.
  3. Write the rules as the text. The whole file then reads: 
    ---
title: Gift wrapping
description: |
  Gift wrapping and gift messages: a shopper asks whether an order can be wrapped, what it costs, or how to add a message. NOT returns of a gift (that is the returns part of the shop guideline).
requires: platform=woocommerce
examples: Can you gift wrap my order?, How much is gift wrapping?, Can I add a gift message?
---
# Gift wrapping

- Gift wrapping costs 4 EUR per order and is offered for every order of physical products.
- When the shopper asks for it, add the product "Gift wrapping" to the cart with
  `woocommerce_cart_add`, then show the basket.

How your store is kept apart

  • Your folder is yours. You can write only in tenants/<your store>/. What belongs to a store is decided by that folder, not by anything written in a file.
  • Your code runs only for you. Your tools and cards are built into a small package of their own, loaded only for your store’s chats, and every tool of yours is limited to your store. Naming another store’s namespace is refused by the build.
  • You see only your platform. Guidelines of another platform, and cards no tool of your platform shows, are not listed and cannot be copied.
  • Settings that grant access are locked. Who may run a tool, its channels, its secrets and the conditions of a guideline are shown locked; a copy may narrow a guideline’s conditions, never widen them.
  • People check code. No tool or card of yours runs anywhere before Codexta has approved its exact code.
